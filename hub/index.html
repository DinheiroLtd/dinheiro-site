Quick reference: FX Glossary

Spot Rate
The exchange rate for a currency trade that settles almost immediately, usually within one or two business days.
Forward Contract
An agreement to exchange currency at a fixed rate on a future date, regardless of how the market moves in between.
Limit Order
An instruction to execute a trade automatically once the market reaches a rate you've specified.
Margin Call
A request for additional funds as collateral on an open forward position when the market has moved against it.
Hedging
Using tools like forward contracts to protect against adverse currency movements rather than speculating on them.
Settlement Date
The date on which currency actually changes hands and funds are delivered to the beneficiary.
Mid-Market Rate
The midpoint between the buy and sell price of a currency pair, often used as a neutral reference rate.
Beneficiary
The person or business receiving the funds at the end of an international payment.
Is Hedging Right for You?

Spot, forward, or limit order: which fits your situation?

A short walkthrough of the three ways to make a currency trade, and when each one tends to make sense. This is general education, not a personal recommendation — your relationship manager can help you decide based on your specific situation.

Best for: paying today

Spot Transaction

Exchange currency at today's live market rate, with same-day or next-day settlement.

  • You need to pay now, or very soon
  • You want simplicity, with no ongoing contract
  • You're comfortable with today's rate
Best for: a known future payment

Forward Contract

Lock in today's rate for a specific future date, so you know exactly what you'll pay or receive.

  • You know the amount and date already
  • You want certainty, not to gamble on the market
  • You want to protect a margin or budget
Best for: a target rate, flexible timing

Limit Order

Set the rate you want and we execute automatically if the market reaches it — no need to watch the screens.

  • You have a rate in mind you'd be happy with
  • You're flexible on exactly when it happens
  • You don't want to monitor the market yourself

FAQs

Questions we get asked most.

The honest, practical answers — transfer timelines, how your money is protected, what documents we need, and how a forward contract actually works.

Most payments settle same-day or next-day, depending on the currency and destination bank, using our global banking network. Your relationship manager will confirm the expected timing when you book a trade, since some currencies and corridors move faster than others.

Payment services for Dinheiro are provided by Sciopay Ltd, which is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority as an Authorised Payment Institution (FRN: 927951). Client funds are held in segregated accounts, separately from Sciopay's own operational money, in line with regulatory safeguarding rules — so your funds remain protected even in the unlikely event Sciopay stopped operating.

Typically, individuals need proof of identity (such as a passport or driving licence) and proof of address, while businesses need company registration details and identification for directors and beneficial owners. For larger or unusual transactions, we may also ask for evidence of source of funds as part of standard anti-money-laundering checks. Your relationship manager will confirm exactly what's needed for your situation.

You agree a future date and amount with your relationship manager, who quotes a rate reflecting today's market plus the forward points for that date. Once you confirm, the rate is locked in for you. Depending on the size and length of the contract, you may be asked for a deposit as security. On the agreed date, you send your funds and Dinheiro settles at the rate you locked in, regardless of where the market has moved since.

A spot trade exchanges currency at today's rate for near-immediate settlement. A forward contract locks in today's rate for a specific date in the future. A limit order sets a target rate and executes automatically once the market reaches it. See the "Is Hedging Right for You?" tab for a fuller walkthrough of when each tends to make sense.
